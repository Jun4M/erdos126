%N Largest cardinality of a set A of distinct positive integers with gcd(A) = 1 such that the product of (a+b)/gcd(a,b) over all pairs a != b in A has at most n distinct prime factors.

%D 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 14, 16, 17, 20

%O 1

%C This is E_2(n) in the notation of Wu (2019), who proved a(1) = 2, a(2) = 4 and E_2(n) <= 2^n.

%C Wu asked (Problem 1) whether E_1(n) = E_2(n) for all n >= 2, where E_1 is the analogous quantity without dividing by the gcd (A______), and also whether the extremal set families always differ. Computation shows that neither alternative holds: E_1 and E_2 differ at n = 3, 4 and 9, but agree at n = 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 and 11. The separation is intermittent, with no evident pattern.

%C For n = 1..11, E_1(n) = 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, 14, 15, 17, 20 while E_2(n) = 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 14, 16, 17, 20.

%C a(3) = 6 is attained by the set of divisors of 63, A = {1,3,7,9,21,63}, with S = {2,5,11}: the values (a+b)/gcd(a,b) are 4, 8, 10, 16, 22, 64. More generally, if A is the full set of divisors of some n, then (a+b)/gcd(a,b) ranges over u+v with u, v coprime divisors of n satisfying uv | n; this collapses C(tau(n),2) conditions down to the number of coprime divisor pairs. E_1 admits no such collapse, which is the structural reason the two functions separate.

%C A second, structurally different witness for a(3) = 6 is {35,49,77,175,275,385} with S = {2,3,31}.

%C a(9) = 16 is attained by {1,3,5,9,13,15,17,21,25,27,29,39,51,63,75,87} with S = {2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23}; all sixteen elements are odd.

%C a(4) = 7 also has at least two witnesses: {2,23,46,138,322,598,782} with S = {2,3,5,7}, and {1,7,14,31,49,119,161} with S = {2,3,5,19}.

%C Wu asked (Problem 3) whether E_2(n+1) = 2*E_2(n) for infinitely many n. In the computed range the doubling occurs only at n = 1 (2 -> 4).

%C Terms are the maxima found by exhaustive search over all A contained in [1,X] and over all n-element prime sets containing 2 (2 must lie in S once |A| >= 3), the pool of primes ranging from {2,3,...,23} to {2,3,...,43} depending on n, with X up to 10000. They are lower bounds in general; values were stable as X increased. All values were independently reproduced by a second implementation using a different clique algorithm and a different smoothness test, and every witness set was re-checked by direct factorisation.

%e a(3) = 6: for A = {1,3,7,9,21,63} the fifteen values (a+b)/gcd(a,b) are 4, 8, 10, 22, 64, 10, 4, 8, 22, 16, 4, 10, 10, 8, 4, involving only the primes 2, 5 and 11.

%H B.-L. Wu, Sumsets with restricted number of prime factors, Lith. Math. J. 59 (2019), 251-260.
